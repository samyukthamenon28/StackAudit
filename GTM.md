# Go-to-Market

## Exact Target User

**Primary**: Engineering manager or CTO at a Series A–C software startup (10–80 engineers), who approved adding AI tools to the stack 6–18 months ago and now has a CFO asking what the ROI is. They're not the one who set up the subscriptions — that was a developer — but they're the one being asked to justify the budget.

**Secondary**: Senior developer or tech lead at the same company who is the informal "AI tools person" and wants to bring a savings memo to their manager. They're the one who will actually run the audit and then forward the share URL upward.

**What disqualifies**: A solo developer paying $20/month (not enough savings to matter) or a large enterprise (procurement processes make ad-hoc tooling audits irrelevant).

## What They're Googling/Reading

Before they'd want this tool, they're reading:
- "How much does Cursor Pro cost for teams" (price comparison searches)
- Posts on Hacker News titled "We cut our AI tool budget by X%"
- The Pragmatic Engineer newsletter's tooling coverage

- CFO/finance blog posts about SaaS spend management

## First 100 Users in 30 Days (Zero Budget)

**Week 1: Hacker News**
Submit "Show HN: I built a free AI tool cost auditor for dev teams" on a Tuesday at 9am ET. The timing matters — peak HN engagement. The title leads with the user benefit, not the technology. Target 30–50 early users from this alone if it gets traction. Even if it doesn't hit front page, the self-post comments are full of people saying "I need this."

**Week 1: Direct outreach**
Cold DM 20 CTOs/engineering managers on LinkedIn with a personalized note: "I saw your team uses [Cursor/Copilot] — I built a tool that audits whether you're on the right plan for your team size. Took me 90 seconds to check my own stack, found $120/mo in savings. [link]" The specificity (mentioning their actual tools from their company's GitHub or job postings) converts. Aim for 5–10 responses.

**Week 2: Community posts**
Post in r/ExperiencedDevs: "I audited my AI tool stack and found $200/mo in waste. Here's what I found and the tool I built to check yours." Personal story format, not promotional. Reddit hates spam but loves tools with genuine savings stories.

**Week 3: Twitter/X engineering accounts**
Post a thread: "I just ran an audit of every AI tool my team pays for. Here's what I found:" — actual data from running the tool on a real stack (with permission or hypothetical). Ends with a link. Tag @cursor_ai, @github if they respond.

**Week 4: The Pragmatic Engineer**
Email Gergely Orosz or his newsletter submission form with a "tool of the week" pitch. One placement in The Pragmatic Engineer hits exactly 100k senior engineers at the right company stage. Single highest-leverage distribution channel available at zero cost.
